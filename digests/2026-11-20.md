#### 1. Assessing Vision-Language Models for Early Prediction of Unsafe Situations in Robotics
Muhammad Farooq et al. · 2026-11-18 · http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24146v1 · track 3 (robotic safety)

- **Contribution (abstract):** An empirical evaluation of whether general-purpose VLMs can predict robot failures *before* an unsafe situation occurs.
- **Approach (abstract):** Qwen, InternVL and LLaVA-OneVision are tested on three robotic case studies (HuRoN, Pal Robotics, CrowdBot), using video and odometry. Situations are classified as safe / potentially unsafe / unsafe, with uncertainty measured by several metrics and related to performance. Qwen is reported strongest overall for classification and reliability; InternVL is reported stronger at early warning in several configurations.
- **Limitations (from abstract):** Gives no headline numbers. Whether robot-state input and temporal history help "depends on the model and evaluation objective", so there is no single best configuration. The case studies are mobile/social-navigation settings, not industrial ones.
- **Relevance (interpretation):** The closest match to the capstone's safety-reasoning question. It gives a ready baseline (VLM-only, three-class safety labels with uncertainty) for comparing against a pipeline of grounded predicates plus verified rules. Experiment: run the same three-class labelling with ILP-learned rules over VLM-extracted predicates, and compare early-warning lead time and calibration.

#### 2. PGX: Physically Grounded Execution for Long-Horizon Humanoid Loco-Manipulation
Kai Lu et al. · 2026-11-19 · http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26596v1 · track 1 (VLM/grounding)

- **Contribution (abstract):** Moves temporal and precision control out of a monolithic VLA into two classical, training-free components, so that the VLA handles only skill dynamics within each stage.
- **Approach (abstract):** Three layers: (i) a predicate-based state machine in which *physical predicates* gate phase transitions, trigger recovery and coordinate navigation and waiting, with shared predicate semantics across demonstration segmentation, online execution and stage-wise evaluation; (ii) VLA skills inside manipulation phases; (iii) world-coordinate locking for spatial consistency. Tested on three simulated tasks and one real task on a Unitree G1.
- **Limitations (from abstract):** Only one real-world task. The abstract reports improvements but gives no numbers. It does not say how the predicates are defined (they appear hand-designed) or how they are grounded.
- **Relevance (interpretation):** A direct architectural precedent for "visually grounded relational predicates gating actions". Experiment: replace PGX-style hand-written transition predicates with ILP-induced ones, and add safety predicates as an extra gating layer that can veto or trigger recovery.

#### 3. FVScore: Specification-Driven Neuro-Symbolic Video Evaluation
Do Xuan Long et al. · 2026-11-17 · http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22673v1 · track 2 (neuro-symbolic)

- **Contribution (abstract):** A neuro-symbolic evaluator that checks videos against explicit and implicit propositions and produces timestamped, evidence-grounded reports of violations.
- **Approach (abstract):** Compiles each video into explicit propositions (from the prompt) and implicit ones (physical, temporal and cross-modal constraints). Each proposition is grounded with specialised perceptual tools, and the confidence traces are scored with soft LTL-inspired temporal operators. A Significance-Aware Calibration step keeps only perceptually significant violations. Reported to beat holistic and agent-based evaluators on correlation with human judgement.
- **Limitations (from abstract):** It targets *generated* video evaluation, not real-world monitoring. The abstract gives no numbers. Implicit propositions are produced automatically, and the abstract does not say how complete or correct they are.
- **Relevance (interpretation):** Its pipeline (proposition → perceptual grounding → temporal-logic check → evidence report) closely mirrors a safety-rule verification pipeline. Experiment: encode industrial safety rules as propositions and reuse the soft temporal operators to flag rule violations in factory video, with timestamped evidence.

#### 4. Reasoning on Call: Efficient Agentic Robot Control for Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation (RoC)
Zilang Cen et al. · 2026-11-19 · http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26341v1 · track 5 (robotics systems)

- **Contribution (abstract):** Decides *when* a high-level reasoning agent should intervene, and whether an error needs execution repair, plan revision or both.
- **Approach (abstract):** An online task-state monitor combines temporal visual observations with ordered interaction memory to estimate subtask progress and predict the next subtask. Event-triggered agent review then repairs or replans, and verified outcomes govern memory updates. On RMBench it reports +24.0 pp success and 65.3% fewer tokens than the strongest agent baseline, and +15.1 pp over the strongest policy baseline. Three real-world tasks are included.
- **Limitations (from abstract):** Evaluated on a single benchmark (RMBench) plus three real tasks. Monitor failure modes, such as missed triggers, are not characterised in the abstract.
- **Relevance (interpretation):** Closely aligned with the planned agentic contribution (diagnose failures, then choose a bounded corrective action from evidence). Its split of corrective actions into execution repair, plan revision or both is a useful template for the action set.

#### 5. Mind2Act World: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Embodied Mind, Physical Action, and Mind-Action Composition in Robotic Manipulation
Yuzhi Huang et al. · 2026-11-19 · http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26444v1 · tracks 1, 5

- **Contribution (abstract):** A benchmark that attributes failures to reasoning ("Mind"), execution ("Act") or their composition.
- **Approach (abstract):** 30 simulated manipulation tasks from five domains, each with 3 difficulty levels and 2 scene settings, for 180 configurations. A diagnosis harness attributes failed episodes and produces per-system profile cards. The strongest agent reaches 52.7% (Mind), 39.0% (Act) and 12.0% (Mind2Act).
- **Limitations (from abstract):** Simulation only. Only three representative systems are diagnosed in depth. The domains are not stated to include safety-critical scenarios.
- **Relevance (interpretation):** Its failure-attribution harness is a model for evaluating a diagnosis-and-correction agent. Experiment: label capstone failures as perception/grounding, rule/reasoning or execution in the same way, and report profile cards.

#### 6. Risk-Controlled Test-Time Adaptation for Vision-Language-Action Models (R2-VLA)
Zixuan Liu et al. · 2026-11-19 · http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26438v1 · tracks 1, 5

- **Contribution (abstract):** A retrieval-augmented VLA that meets a user-specified task-success requirement while asking for as little expert help as possible.
- **Approach (abstract):** A learned uncertainty predictor decides whether to act or request expert help. A task-level risk-control procedure calibrates the threshold to a failure-risk target, and expert corrections are added to retrieval memory while the guarantee is preserved. Reports +9.2 to +11.2 pp average success across three VLA backbones and four LIBERO suites.
- **Limitations (from abstract):** LIBERO simulation only. The guarantee concerns task failure, not physical safety. It relies on available expert assistance.
- **Relevance (interpretation):** It offers a principled, calibrated "act or escalate" decision. That fits bounded corrective actions: abstaining or escalating to a human could be one of the actions, with its threshold set by risk control instead of tuned by hand.

#### 7. Worth Another Look: Constrained World-Model Planning for Visual Inspection
Ziyue Kang et al. · 2026-11-18 · http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24187v1 · track 2 (neuro-symbolic)

- **Contribution (abstract):** Treats multi-target visual inspection over frozen detectors as neuro-symbolic observation planning under compute and sensing budgets.
- **Approach (abstract):** A compact recurrent world model is decoupled from exact symbolic feasibility and tariff rules. Receding-horizon beam search schedules class queries, crops, enhancements and stopping, with slack-aware shadow pricing. Reports up to +28.4 AP pp on RUOD and +15.1 on ARIS over static priority, with 11 ms planning cost, across eight terrestrial, optical and sonar benchmarks.
- **Limitations (from abstract):** Gains are largest under tight budgets. Detectors are frozen, so detector errors are not corrected. The symbolic rules are budget/tariff constraints, not semantic safety rules.
- **Relevance (interpretation):** Suggests an "active re-query" corrective action: when grounding evidence for a safety predicate is weak, the agent could plan a crop, enhancement or re-query rather than decide immediately. That makes "look again" a bounded, budgeted action.

#### 8. Stuck in Place: Stall Diagnosis and Remedies for JEPA Planners
Junhyeok Lee et al. · 2026-11-19 · http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25415v1 · track 4 (robot learning/control)

- **Contribution (abstract):** Shows that most failures of JEPA world-model MPC planners on pushing tasks (at least 96%) are *stalls*: the object stays in place while the model predicts progress. These failures are a fixed point of the planning loop, not just model error under distribution shift.
- **Approach (abstract):** Traces the executed planning loop. Proposes Unstick: on-policy fine-tuning before deployment, plus a fixed-weight loop breaker (a residual memory of prediction errors, or random re-initialization at each replan). Code is linked in the abstract.
- **Limitations (from abstract):** Pushing tasks only. The re-initialization remedy recovers much less of the gain under visual shifts. The analysis is specific to the warm-started gradient-descent planner.
- **Relevance (interpretation):** A clean case study in diagnosing the cause of a failure from evidence before choosing a remedy. It also shows that test-time adaptation can be attributed to the wrong cause. Methodologically, it backs the capstone's planned "diagnose, then select a bounded correction" approach.

#### Synthesis (interpretation)

- **Predicates as the control interface.** PGX (physical predicates gate stage transitions), FVScore (propositions grounded by perceptual tools and checked with temporal logic) and Farooq et al. (safe/potentially unsafe/unsafe labels from VLMs) each point to a layer between perception and action. The capstone could own that layer with learned, verified rules. A concrete combined experiment: FVScore-style temporal checking of ILP-learned safety predicates, evaluated on Farooq et al.'s early-warning protocol.
- **Diagnose before correcting.** RoC (event-triggered repair vs. replanning), Mind2Act (failure attribution to reasoning, execution or composition) and Stuck in Place (stall vs. model error) all argue that the right correction depends on correctly attributing the failure. Together they suggest an evaluation design for the planned agentic contribution: attribution accuracy first, corrective success second.
- **Calibrated, bounded actions.** R2-VLA (risk-controlled escalation) and Worth Another Look (budgeted re-query) supply two concrete bounded actions, "escalate" and "look again", each with a principled trigger.

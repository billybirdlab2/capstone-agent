#### 1. SymAnt: Grounding Long-Horizon Action Anticipation with Adaptable Symbolic Priors
Fnu Aryan et al. · 2026-10-29 · http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40632v1 · track 2 (neuro-symbolic)

- **Contribution (abstract):** Neuro-symbolic long-horizon action anticipation from short video; users can edit the underlying knowledge to adjust predictions without retraining.
- **Approach (abstract):** Joint search over a scene graph and a knowledge graph builds a symbolic context (spatial relations, attributes, affordances, actions) used as a prior for iterative refinement of the predicted action sequence. Evaluated on Breakfast, 50 Salads, EPIC-KITCHENS, EGTEA Gaze+ and Assembly101.
- **Limitations (from abstract):** Advantage is stated mainly for *short* observation contexts; no quantitative margins given in the abstract; depends on the availability/quality of a knowledge graph and scene-graph extraction.
- **Relevance (interpretation):** Bridges the VLM/video-understanding and neuro-symbolic tracks. A natural experiment: replace the hand-built knowledge graph with rules learned by ILP / predicate invention from demonstrations, and test whether learned priors match hand-authored ones.

#### 2. PhysVLMM: Interaction-Conditioned Physical Belief Maps for Articulated Objects in Robot Scene Maps
Dibyendu Ghosh · 2026-10-29 · http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40539v1 · track 1 (VLM/grounding)

- **Contribution (abstract):** Maintains a physical belief (posterior) for every articulated part in a Vision-Language-Motion Map and answers planner queries about whether an action will succeed at a stated risk, or abstains.
- **Approach (abstract):** Map-update and query algorithms; keeps an unidentifiable direction as a posterior; passes evidence from each pull in closed form to untouched look-alike parts. Reported: joint type identified in 86% of held-out pulls (vs 48% for a learned single-view method); 41% lower response-prediction error than differentiable simulation (CRPS 0.323 vs 0.544) at 13 ms vs 230 s per case; false-safe answers stay within stated risk 0.02–0.30. Real doors/drawers in 18 homes.
- **Limitations (from abstract):** Restricted to doors and drawers; the abstract itself notes that simpler variants predict stop position at least as well, and pooling all parts does as well as look-alike grouping, so the sharing mechanism's benefit is partial.
- **Relevance (interpretation):** Strongest single fit to "grounding + safety": a risk-calibrated, abstaining world model attached to a semantic map. Candidate experiment: use its abstention signal as a hazard gate for manipulation in the safety track.

#### 3. world2vec: Mechanistic world models through latent dynamics learning
Tobias Schmidt et al. · 2026-10-29 · http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40637v1 · track 4 (robot learning/control)

- **Contribution (abstract):** Learns reward-free latent dynamics from observations and actions that faithfully reproduce the true system over long horizons, aimed at interpretability and scientific discovery.
- **Approach (abstract):** Latent dynamics learning with at least an order of magnitude fewer parameters than DreamerV3, TD-MPC2 and LeWM; reports state-of-the-art pixel-based continuous control and system identification across 71 attractors.
- **Limitations (from abstract):** Short abstract with no method details; evaluation is on continuous benchmark environments and attractor systems, not physical robots; "mechanistic" faithfulness criteria are not defined in the abstract.
- **Relevance (interpretation):** A compact, faithful dynamics model is directly usable inside MPC. Experiment: swap it in as the prediction model for an MPC loop and compare constraint violation vs. a TD-MPC2 baseline.

#### 4. Learning Safe Flow Matching Robot Policies from Binary Feasibility Oracles
Jianming Ma et al. · 2026-10-29 · http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40075v1 · track 5 (robotics systems)

- **Contribution (abstract):** Safe flow-matching manipulation policies when constraints are available only as black-box binary feasibility queries.
- **Approach (abstract):** Oracle-guided differentiable safeguard: corrects unsafe updates in the forward pass and extracts local boundary information for constraint-aware gradients in the backward pass; same safeguard at training and inference with discrete-time flow matching. Reports 100% constraint satisfaction on obstacle-aware manipulation while preserving task performance.
- **Limitations (from abstract):** Evaluation scope limited to obstacle-aware manipulation; oracle query cost and latency are not discussed in the abstract; constraints are joint limits/collision, not semantic hazards.
- **Relevance (interpretation):** Plug-in safety layer for VLA/flow policies. Experiment: use a VLM-based hazard detector (e.g. track 1 outputs) as the binary oracle and measure whether the guarantee degrades with a noisy oracle.

#### 5. Teaching a Tree to Recover: Online Distillation of an LLM Supervisor with Counterfactual Replay
Kristian Ceder et al. · 2026-10-29 · http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39775v1 · track 4

- **Contribution (abstract):** Distills an LLM supervisor (replan / wait / call for help) for an MPC-based mobile robot online into a decision tree, removing LLM queries at test time.
- **Approach (abstract):** Counterfactual replay labels recoveries as needed / unnecessary / harmful, retiring unhelpful rules. In simulation, on 20 unseen layouts where MPC alone freezes, the tree handles at least 18 correctly across all seeds, matching an always-query LLM and expert rules.
- **Limitations (from abstract):** Simulation only; small evaluation set (20 layouts); decision-tree expressiveness may limit relational generalization.
- **Relevance (interpretation):** Very close to ILP: LLM-as-teacher for symbolic rule induction. Experiment: replace the decision tree with an ILP learner (relational rules with invented predicates) and compare rule count and transfer to new layouts.

#### 6. Assuring Learning-Enabled Cyber-Physical Systems with Foundation Models: A Neuro-Symbolic Agenda for Testing, Verification, and Runtime Assurance
Xi Zheng et al. · 2026-10-29 · http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39547v1 · track 2

- **Contribution (abstract):** Position/agenda paper proposing a "Foundation Model Assurance Stack" for CPS such as autonomous vehicles and UAVs.
- **Approach (abstract):** Three directions: neuro-symbolic architectures constraining foundation models via symbolic world models; conformance-based testing with formal specs and FM-assisted spec mining / scenario generation; compositional verification and runtime monitoring/enforcement.
- **Limitations (from abstract):** Agenda, not an empirical study; no results reported.
- **Relevance (interpretation):** Useful framing for the safety-verification track and for aerial robotics; a map for positioning items 2, 4 and 5 (runtime assurance, safeguards, symbolic supervisors).

#### 7. Learned Terminal Costs for Short-Horizon Model Predictive Control of Autonomous Trucks in Interactive Highway Traffic
Hooman Hamze et al. · 2026-10-29 · http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40257v1 · track 4

- **Contribution (abstract):** Hybrid MPC + RL in which a pre-trained RL model provides the terminal cost for a short-horizon MPC solved by dynamic programming.
- **Approach (abstract):** Compared against pure MPC (zero terminal cost), rollout with a base-policy terminal estimate, and pure RL in simulation with reactive traffic; the hybrid reports zero collisions with the lowest cost of any configuration.
- **Limitations (from abstract):** Simulation only; domain is highway trucking; no formal safety guarantee claimed for the learned terminal cost.
- **Relevance (interpretation):** Template for combining MBRL value functions with MPC on a robot (e.g. aerial or climbing platform), where horizon length is compute-limited.

#### 8. Interpretable Meta-Policies for Neural Decision Making (NEXUS)
Raban Emunds et al. · 2026-10-27 · http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37451v1 · track 2

- **Contribution (abstract):** Hierarchical RL with neural skills and symbolic meta-policies, moving interpretability to the skill level.
- **Approach (abstract):** Object-centric observations; reward functions and meta-policies designed by interaction with LLMs; compares neural, symbolic and neuro-symbolic meta-policies. Reports disentangled skills, better goal alignment, less reward hacking and more robustness to game simplifications than deep RL.
- **Limitations (from abstract):** Evaluated on games (references a "game manual"); no robotics experiments; no quantitative figures in the abstract.
- **Relevance (interpretation):** Symbolic meta-policies over neural skills are a natural target for ILP-learned rules; could be transferred to a manipulation skill library (e.g. SwapBot-style tool skills).

#### Synthesis (interpretation)
- **Symbolic layers over neural components** is the dominant theme: knowledge-graph priors for video (SymAnt), distilled rule supervisors for MPC (Teaching a Tree), symbolic meta-policies (NEXUS), and the assurance stack (Zheng et al.). The common open question — *where do the rules come from?* — is exactly where ILP / predicate invention could contribute; papers 5 and 8 are the most direct testbeds.
- **Calibrated safety at the action level:** PhysVLMM (risk-bounded abstention) and the feasibility-oracle safeguard both turn uncertain perception or black-box constraints into enforceable action filters. Combining them (VLM-grounded belief as the oracle) is a concrete experiment.
- **Compact dynamics for control:** world2vec and learned terminal costs both target the model/horizon trade-off in MPC; together they suggest a small-model MBRL + short-horizon MPC pipeline for compute-limited aerial or climbing robots.
